Candidate Modules: what each one looks like, and what it does to the field

Eight proposed Modules, drawn to the project's real scale. Each panel shows the geometry plus the marble paths through it; the strip underneath shows what it does to marble order — which is the whole reason a Role exists. None of these are built. Every dimension is a design proposal, not a measurement.

Build first — the four that fill a Role cleanly

One per obstacle Role. Together they take the Assembler from 1 possible Role selection to 16.

Crossover weave

shuffle
Plan view — looking at the Board face-on from above; travel left→right, channel width top↔bottom

What you'd see: two channels braid past each other in the middle of the Module. A marble that entered on the near lane leaves on the far lane, and vice versa. Nothing moves — the swap is pure geometry, so it never jams and never stalls.

Moving parts
None. step() returns [].
Dwell
Bounded by path length; no basin, no meeting point.
Params
Lane count (2–3), crossing length, bridge rise (≈40 mm — clears a 32 mm marble underneath).
Effect on the field — order inverts, gaps unchanged

Turnstile

queue
Elevation — the viewer's face-on view; travel left→right, gravity down

What you'd see: the pack piles up against a slowly spinning cross, and each quarter turn lets exactly one marble drop through onto the exit ramp. Maximum suspense per unit of geometry: you can count the marbles waiting. Unlike the windmill, which bats marbles unpredictably, the turnstile admits them at a fixed rate.

Moving parts
One kinematic hub — reuses KinematicRotationMotion unchanged.
Dwell
Bounded: worst case ≈ (field size ÷ arms) × rotation period.
Params
Arm count (3–4), angular velocity, throat width, hub radius.
Effect on the field — order preserved, gaps forced open

Bumper cluster

scatter
Elevation — the viewer's face-on view; travel left→right, gravity down

What you'd see: a handful of big pinball bumpers in an open chamber. Few contacts, each one violent and unmistakable. Where the pin field diffuses the pack gently over dozens of tiny taps, this throws two neighbours to opposite walls in one hit — the same Role, the opposite texture.

Moving parts
None.
Dwell
Bounded — every surface is convex, so there is no pocket to settle in.
Params
Bumper count (4–6), radii, restitution (proposed 0.6–0.8 vs. the 0.15 track default).
Effect on the field — order scrambled and spread out

Cascading ledges

sort
Elevation — the viewer's face-on view; travel left→right, gravity down

What you'd see: a staircase with the risers removed. A marble carrying speed launches off the first ledge and flies the whole flight in one arc; a slow one drops onto every shelf and crawls. The sorting criterion is visible — you can see who flew, which is exactly what friction-lanes could never show.

Moving parts
None.
Dwell
Bounded — every ledge is a downhill surface, so nothing can rest.
Params
Ledge count (3–5), ledge length, gap width, drop per ledge, ledge tilt.
Effect on the field — reordered by incoming speed, not by luck

Held back — good ideas with an unresolved problem

Each of these works on paper. Each also has one specific thing that has to be settled before it is safe to build.

Splitter tree

scatter
Plan view — looking at the Board face-on from above; travel left→right, channel width top↔bottom

What you'd see: a Galton board built from seven big wedges instead of a hundred pins. Each marble takes three visible left-or-right decisions and lands in one of eight lanes. The randomness reads as a sequence of choices rather than as noise.

Unresolved: a 32 mm marble arriving dead-centre on a sharp wedge apex can balance there indefinitely — an unbounded Dwell Time hiding inside otherwise safe geometry. Round the apex to a radius the marble cannot rest on, or bias every wedge off-centre, before building.

Pocketed drum

shuffle
Elevation — the viewer's face-on view; travel left→right, gravity down

What you'd see: the classic marble-run crowd-pleaser. Marbles get scooped into rim pockets, carried up and over, and tipped out in an order set by which pocket caught them. Highly watchable, and it reuses the same rotation motion the windmill already has.

Unresolved: with 6 pockets and a field of 15, nine marbles wait outside the drum on every pass — so it behaves as much like a Queue as a Shuffle. Either widen it until pockets ≥ field size, or measure it in the Validator and register it under whichever Role it actually earns.

Zipper merge

queue
Plan view — looking at the Board face-on from above; travel left→right, channel width top↔bottom

What you'd see: two lanes combing into one, alternating strictly, so the field leaves in single file. The drama is watching which of two neighbours takes the gap.

Unresolved: this is funnel-choke's geometry with a comb bolted on. It is only safe if the merge zone provably cannot hold two marbles abreast at any point. If you cannot show that from the geometry, you are re-shipping the Module you just removed — skip it.

Loaded rocker

sort
Elevation — the viewer's face-on view; travel left→right, gravity down

What you'd see: a see-saw that holds marbles until their combined weight tips it, then dumps the load and swings back. Genuinely the most dramatic thing on this list.

Unresolved — this one is a contract change, not a Module. The tilt is driven by marble contacts, so step(spec, tSeconds) could no longer be pure in t. It needs a real dynamic body, which means the Validator's world and the renderer's world stop being the same world. Decide that on purpose, in an ADR, before anyone writes the geometry.

Scale and conventions. Every panel is drawn from the constants in src/race/scale.ts — 32 mm marble, 0.5 m channel, 0.1 m Cell pitch — and Module travel spans are consistent with the 0.6–1.8 m range the live Modules occupy. Plan views look at the Board face-on from above, so the vertical axis is channel width; elevations are the viewer's face-on view, so the vertical axis is gravity.
