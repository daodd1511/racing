Candidate Modules: what each one looks like, and what it does to the field

Nineteen proposed Modules, drawn to the project's real scale. Each panel shows the geometry plus the marble paths through it; the strip underneath shows what it does to marble order — which is the whole reason a Role exists. None of these are built. Every dimension is a design proposal, not a measurement.

One Module per obstacle Role means enumerateRoleSelections() returns exactly 1 Course. A second Module for each of scatter, shuffle and sort takes that to 8; a third to 27. That multiplication is the reason to build more than one of these.

Build first — the four that fill a Role cleanly

One per obstacle Role as the Roles stood on 2026-08-24. Three of the four still land on a live Role and together take the Assembler from 1 possible Role selection to 8; the Turnstile's queue no longer exists.

Crossover weave

shuffle
Plan view — looking at the Board face-on from above; travel left→right, channel width top↔bottom

What you'd see: two channels braid past each other in the middle of the Module. A marble that entered on the near lane leaves on the far lane, and vice versa. Nothing moves — the swap is pure geometry, so it never jams and never stalls.

Moving parts
None. step() returns [].
Dwell
Bounded by path length; no basin, no meeting point.
Params
Lane count (2–3), crossing length, bridge rise (≈40 mm — clears a 32 mm marble underneath).
Effect on the field — order inverts, gaps unchanged

Turnstile

queue
Elevation — the viewer's face-on view; travel left→right, gravity down

What you'd see: the pack piles up against a slowly spinning cross, and each quarter turn lets exactly one marble drop through onto the exit ramp. Maximum suspense per unit of geometry: you can count the marbles waiting. Unlike the windmill, which bats marbles unpredictably, the turnstile admits them at a fixed rate.

Moving parts
One kinematic hub — reuses KinematicRotationMotion unchanged.
Dwell
Bounded: worst case ≈ (field size ÷ arms) × rotation period.
Params
Arm count (3–4), angular velocity, throat width, hub radius.
Changed since this was drawn: there is no queue Role any more. It left the Role union with the windmill in 2be90ca, so the Turnstile now costs a Role addition — src/modules/types.ts, the Arc's Slot table in src/course/arc.ts, and ROLE_ORDER — before its geometry is worth writing. Registering it as sort instead is the cheaper reading: forced gaps between marbles is what sort is already for.
Effect on the field — order preserved, gaps forced open

Bumper cluster

scatter
Elevation — the viewer's face-on view; travel left→right, gravity down

What you'd see: a handful of big pinball bumpers in an open chamber. Few contacts, each one violent and unmistakable. Where the pin field diffuses the pack gently over dozens of tiny taps, this throws two neighbours to opposite walls in one hit — the same Role, the opposite texture.

Moving parts
None.
Dwell
Bounded — every surface is convex, so there is no pocket to settle in.
Params
Bumper count (4–6), radii, restitution (proposed 0.6–0.8 vs. the 0.15 track default).
Effect on the field — order scrambled and spread out

Cascading ledges

sort
Elevation — the viewer's face-on view; travel left→right, gravity down

What you'd see: a staircase with the risers removed. A marble carrying speed launches off the first ledge and flies the whole flight in one arc; a slow one drops onto every shelf and crawls. The sorting criterion is visible — you can see who flew, which is exactly what friction-lanes could never show.

Moving parts
None.
Dwell
Bounded — every ledge is a downhill surface, so nothing can rest.
Params
Ledge count (3–5), ledge length, gap width, drop per ledge, ledge tilt.
Effect on the field — reordered by incoming speed, not by luck

More candidates — scatter

Everything from here down is drawn against the Course as it actually runs: a 0.5 m channel at COURSE_GRADE 0.12, entry and exit on the same centreline, and no free fall between Modules. Static geometry unless a card says otherwise — the windmill was the one moving Module that shipped, and it came out again.

Herringbone ribs

scatter
Plan view — looking at the Board face-on from above; travel left→right, channel width top↔bottom

What you'd see: a floor of angled ribs, like a herringbone parquet. Each rib gives the marble a small sideways nudge in a direction the rib chooses, so the field fans across the full channel width without a single vertical obstacle. It is the pin field's effect delivered by the floor instead of by posts — nothing to wedge between, nothing to balance on.

Moving parts
None. step() returns [].
Dwell
Strictly bounded: no rib is tall enough to stop a marble, so travel time is path length over speed.
Params
Rib count (6–14), rib angle (20–35°), rib height (6–10 mm), pitch.
Watch: rib height is the whole design. Above ~12 mm (0.75 marble radius) a rib stops deflecting and starts acting as a step, which is the Staircase's job and hurts speed badly at a 12% grade.

Baffle slalom

scatter
Plan view — looking at the Board face-on from above; travel left→right, channel width top↔bottom

What you'd see: a slalom. Two marbles entering side by side take visibly different lines through it — the one that gets the inside of each turn walks away, and which marble that is depends only on where it happened to be when it arrived. Big lateral spread out of a Module barely longer than the Chute.

Moving parts
None.
Dwell
Bounded, but longer than the Chute it replaces — every marble travels ~1.3× the straight distance.
Params
Baffle count (3–6), coverage (0.5–0.7 of channel width), pitch, baffle thickness.
Watch: coverage is a choke in disguise. Past ~0.75 the gap drops under four marble diameters and a 15-marble pack backs up — the exact failure that got the funnel choke cut. Cap the parameter in the schema, do not leave it to tuning.

Kicker lip

scatter
Elevation — the viewer's face-on view; travel left→right, gravity down

What you'd see: the only jump in the catalogue. Marbles leave the floor for about a fifth of a second and land spread out along the run-out by arrival speed. It converts a speed difference too small to see into a gap of several marble diameters, and it is the best-looking single shot available on a flat Board.

Moving parts
None.
Dwell
Bounded and short — ballistic, no contact.
Params
Lip rise (24–48 mm), lip radius, run-out length, roof clearance.
Unresolved: an airborne marble is the one thing this Course has no containment story for. Everything else is a channel with rails. Settle the roof geometry and prove it in the Validator across the full entry-speed range before the lip, not after.

More candidates — shuffle

Reordering the field is the property that makes the race feel unrigged, and Whoops is currently the only Module that does it. These three do it by three different mechanisms.

Drop shelves

shuffle
Elevation — the viewer's face-on view; travel left→right, gravity down

What you'd see: a switchback in miniature, but the point is not the descent — it is that a marble's order at the end of a shelf has nothing to do with its order at the start. Marbles bunch at the shelf lip, tip off in whatever order the bunching produced, and get one more chance to reorder on each of the next two shelves.

Moving parts
None. This is the Staircase's geometry turned 90° in intent: it reorders instead of spacing out.
Dwell
Bounded; every shelf slopes, so nothing has a flat spot to rest on.
Params
Shelf count (2–4), shelf grade, drop between shelves (40–80 mm), overlap.
Watch: the lip. A square shelf edge catches a marble on its shoulder and stalls it; bevel the last 20 mm, and keep the drop under four marble diameters so the landing does not launch it off the shelf below.

Unequal twin loop

shuffle
Plan view — looking at the Board face-on from above; travel left→right, channel width top↔bottom

What you'd see: the channel opens into two visibly unequal lanes and closes again. Every marble that takes the long side loses about a fifth of a second, so the leader arriving on the wrong side of the splitter comes out behind. Spectators can see the outcome coming from the moment of the split, which is the whole appeal.

Moving parts
None. Pure geometry, so it can never jam or stall.
Dwell
Two fixed values, one per lane. The most predictable Module in this document.
Params
Length ratio (1.2–1.6), splitter offset (biases how the field divides), lane width.
Watch: each lane is half the channel — 0.25 m, about eight marble diameters. That is enough for a 15-marble pack, but the splitter apex has the Splitter tree's balancing problem in miniature. Round it, or offset it so dead centre is never a stable rest.

Catch cups

shuffle
Elevation — the viewer's face-on view; travel left→right, gravity down

What you'd see: marbles dropping into shallow scoops and sitting there, visibly stuck, until a following marble knocks them on. It produces the largest reordering per centimetre of any static idea here, and it reads instantly on camera.

Unresolved — the tail of the field. A cup that only empties on impact has unbounded Dwell Time for whichever marble is last, which is exactly the case last Selection Mode cares most about. Make every cup drain under gravity alone within a fixed time (a lip lower than the entry side), then the mechanic survives; otherwise it is a race that never finishes.

More candidates — sort

Sort spreads the field out by speed so the finish is not a blob. The Staircase does it by interrupting descent; these do it by making speed itself decide the path.

Grate express

sort
Elevation, cut through the channel — travel left→right, gravity down

What you'd see: a cattle grid that sorts. Arrive fast and you fly over the slots and keep the express floor; arrive slow and the first slot swallows you into a longer lane underneath. The gap it opens is proportional to the speed difference that already existed, which is exactly what sort is for — and it does it without costing the leaders anything.

Moving parts
None.
Dwell
Bounded on both paths; the lower lane is longer by construction, not by chance.
Params
Slot count (3–5), slot width (1.05–1.3 marble ⌀), slot pitch, lower-lane drop.
Watch: slot width is a narrow band. Under 1.05 ⌀ nothing ever drops through; over ~1.4 ⌀ everything does and there is no sort, only a detour. Sweep it in the Showcase against real entry speeds before fixing a default.

Split fork, unequal surfaces

sort
Plan view — looking at the Board face-on from above; travel left→right, channel width top↔bottom

What you'd see: two identical-looking lanes with different floors, and a marble that drew the rough one falling behind for the whole length of the Module with no contact drama at all. Cheapest Module in this document to build: two floor plates, one wedge, one material difference, no new geometry emitter and no new shape.

Moving parts
None.
Dwell
Bounded; the rough lane is slower by a fixed ratio, not by an amount that can grow.
Params
Rough-lane friction (0.2–0.6), fork offset, fork length.
Note: this is the lane comb and the friction patches from the 2026-08-18 obstacle list, merged and shrunk to one Module. Both were cut then for reasons that no longer apply — they were designed against an 11 m bed with a fraction placement API. At 0.5 m of channel the two lanes fit side by side with room for the pack.

Counter-slope gate

sort
Elevation — the viewer's face-on view; travel left→right, gravity down

What you'd see: a hill the marbles have to earn. It is the sharpest sort available — it does not slow the tail of the field, it holds it — and the near misses are genuinely tense to watch.

Unresolved — Dwell Time is unbounded by design. With restitution 0.15 and friction 0.08 a marble that fails the crest keeps almost all its energy and oscillates. It escapes only when a follower pushes it, which makes the last marble's finish time unbounded and the race's duration a function of nothing you control. It needs a bleed — a downstream leak from the dip, or a crest that lowers behind the pack — and that is a design decision, not a parameter.

More candidates — accel

The Chute is fixed into every third Arc Slot, which means the Course's connective tissue is the same shot eight times. Neither of these changes what an accel Slot does; they change what it looks like.

Steep zigzag

accel
Elevation — the viewer's face-on view; travel left→right, gravity down

What you'd see: the pack folding back on itself down a set of steep ramps, picking up more speed per Cell of Board than a straight Chute can. It also spreads the field a little at each reversal, so an accel Slot stops being dead time between obstacles.

Moving parts
None.
Dwell
Bounded and shorter than the Chute it replaces at equal drop.
Params
Leg count (2–4), leg grade (0.25–0.45), reversal radius, width.
Note: this Module shipped once and was cut in fe48df4 along with four others — for catalogue size, not for a physics failure. Rebuilding it is a revert plus a re-tune, which makes it the cheapest item on this page. Check the reversals against the connector work in src/course/connectors.ts: hairpins at the Board's row ends already solve the same problem, and the two should share geometry rather than each having their own.

Banked S-bend

accel
Plan view with a section — travel left→right; the inset is cut across the channel

What you'd see: the marbles leaning into a banked curve instead of running straight. It holds speed through a direction change the flat Chute cannot make at all, and the pack strings out along the bank by entry speed — high and fast on the outside, low and slow on the inside.

Moving parts
None.
Dwell
Bounded; comparable to a Chute of the same path length.
Params
Bend radius, bank angle (30–45°), length, width.
Watch: ADR 0003 binds this one. The bank is a curved surface, so it collides as a fan of flat cuboid plates and only the visual is swept — the same treatment Whoops gets. Too few plates and the marble reads every facet edge as a kick; that is the defect src/course/collisionContinuity.test.ts exists to catch.

Held back — good ideas with an unresolved problem

The original four. Each works on paper, and each has one specific thing that has to be settled before it is safe to build. Two of the newer candidates above — Catch cups and the Counter-slope gate — carry the same warning band for the same reason: an unbounded Dwell Time hiding inside geometry that otherwise looks safe.

Splitter tree

scatter
Plan view — looking at the Board face-on from above; travel left→right, channel width top↔bottom

What you'd see: a Galton board built from seven big wedges instead of a hundred pins. Each marble takes three visible left-or-right decisions and lands in one of eight lanes. The randomness reads as a sequence of choices rather than as noise.

Unresolved: a 32 mm marble arriving dead-centre on a sharp wedge apex can balance there indefinitely — an unbounded Dwell Time hiding inside otherwise safe geometry. Round the apex to a radius the marble cannot rest on, or bias every wedge off-centre, before building.

Pocketed drum

shuffle
Elevation — the viewer's face-on view; travel left→right, gravity down

What you'd see: the classic marble-run crowd-pleaser. Marbles get scooped into rim pockets, carried up and over, and tipped out in an order set by which pocket caught them. Highly watchable, and it reuses the same rotation motion the windmill already has.

Unresolved: with 6 pockets and a field of 15, nine marbles wait outside the drum on every pass — so it behaves as much like a Queue as a Shuffle. Either widen it until pockets ≥ field size, or measure it in the Validator and register it under whichever Role it actually earns.

Zipper merge

queue
Plan view — looking at the Board face-on from above; travel left→right, channel width top↔bottom

What you'd see: two lanes combing into one, alternating strictly, so the field leaves in single file. The drama is watching which of two neighbours takes the gap.

Unresolved: this is funnel-choke's geometry with a comb bolted on. It is only safe if the merge zone provably cannot hold two marbles abreast at any point. If you cannot show that from the geometry, you are re-shipping the Module you just removed — skip it.

And: its queue Role no longer exists — see the Turnstile.

Loaded rocker

sort
Elevation — the viewer's face-on view; travel left→right, gravity down

What you'd see: a see-saw that holds marbles until their combined weight tips it, then dumps the load and swings back. Genuinely the most dramatic thing on this list.

Unresolved — this one is a contract change, not a Module. The tilt is driven by marble contacts, so step(spec, tSeconds) could no longer be pure in t. It needs a real dynamic body, which means the Validator's world and the renderer's world stop being the same world. Decide that on purpose, in an ADR, before anyone writes the geometry.

For comparison — what is actually on the Board

Same drawing conventions, so you can judge whether a candidate actually looks and behaves differently from what races already show. Four Modules are registered in src/course/courseModules.ts: the structural Chute plus one obstacle per Role. The windmill is drawn here because it is the one moving Module this project shipped and then removed — read it as the evidence behind every "static geometry preferred" line above.

Scale and conventions. Every panel is drawn from the constants in src/race/scale.ts — 32 mm marble, 0.5 m channel, 0.1 m Cell pitch — and Module travel spans are consistent with the 0.6–1.8 m range the live Modules occupy. Plan views look at the Board face-on from above, so the vertical axis is channel width; elevations are the viewer's face-on view, so the vertical axis is gravity.
Status. All nineteen candidates are unbuilt proposals — eight from the 2026-08-24 brainstorm, eleven added 2026-08-25. Dimensions, pocket counts, friction and restitution values, and every dwell claim are design intent, not Validator measurements — run each through the Validator before trusting any of them. Live Module drawings are schematic, not traced from their buildSpec output.
What changed on 2026-08-25. The catalogue was cut to four obstacles in fe48df4 (steep zigzag, rumble strip, friction lanes, funnel choke, vortex bowl removed) and the windmill followed in 2be90ca, taking the queue Role with it. So the Arc now has exactly one Module per Role and enumerateRoleSelections() yields one Course. The eleven new candidates are drawn against that Course — 0.5 m channel, COURSE_GRADE 0.12, entry and exit on one continuous centreline — rather than against the Showcase, which is why none of them ask for a free fall, a wider bed, or a second Role.
