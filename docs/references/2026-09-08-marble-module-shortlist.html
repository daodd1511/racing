Decision pass · video compared with current code

Keep two ideas that could change the race

Prototype a curved pocket and alternating banked ramps. Hold a simplified split island in reserve; remove five redundant or low-priority sections from the candidate shortlist.

Selection question: Does this create a distinct, readable chance to catch up or pass while keeping marbles moving?

Different scenery is not enough. A candidate must add behavior beyond our existing Modules, work in a compact downhill Course section, and avoid turning the race into a queue. These are design judgments and testable hypotheses, not measured improvements.

01

Curved pocket with an offset exit

Keep · first prototype

Reference windows: 00:02–00:05; also 00:40–00:43

Why it survives the cut

A temporary gathering area could let trailing marbles catch up while earlier arrivals find the exit. That is a different racing moment from repeated Pin field rebounds or Whoops speed changes.

What we would build

Adapt the banks into a continuously draining pocket with room to pass. Keep the offset exit wide enough to avoid a single-file queue. This is an adaptation of the observed shape, not a verified bowl or funnel.

Acceptance condition: Reject it if it merely stops the field, always favors one entry side, or preserves the same exit order after a delay. The intended effect is catch-up with continued motion.

03

Alternating banked ramps

Keep · second prototype

Reference windows: 00:09–00:13; also 01:25–01:27

Why it survives the cut

A marble may ride a bank while another takes a lower path around its tip. Competing paths and sideways motion could make passing readable. Pin field mainly provides repeated post contacts; Staircase and Whoops mainly vary height along travel.

What we would build

Preserve the three-dimensional bank surfaces and open alternatives. Do not reduce it to alternating vertical walls: that would overlap with the Pin field’s existing alternating side bumpers.

Acceptance condition: Reject it if every marble is forced into the same queue or if the result is just another sequence of impacts. High and low paths must remain viable across normal entry speeds.

04

Split island, adapted to split and rejoin

Reserve · outside the immediate shortlist

Reference windows: 00:11–00:16; also 01:27

Why it survives the cut

The useful idea is two paths that rejoin with different arrival timings. A decorative island alone is just a large blocker, and a drop alone overlaps with Staircase.

What we would build

If the first two prototypes leave a gap, try a compact island with both paths joining on one continuous receiving surface. Remove the separate airborne drop from this candidate. That simplified merge is a proposal, not an exact copy of the video.

Acceptance condition: Keep it on hold until both branches can be useful without one becoming a permanent shortcut. Separated paths also need scrutiny under our single-route progress projection.

07 · Winding return lanes Cut from this shortlist Guided routing overlaps with connectors; repeated deflection overlaps with Pin field. 08 · Two boulders and finish queue Cut Boulders repeat blocker behavior; we already have a photo-finish sensor and catch wall. A single-file collector restricts the late passing we want. Keep finish detection and post-finish collection as infrastructure, rather than adding them as an Obstacle Module.

Prototype order and acceptance

1. Curved pocket. Test whether trailing marbles can close a gap while early arrivals continue moving toward the exit. This targets a behavior missing from our current catalogue: a shared area that temporarily brings the field closer together.

2. Alternating banked ramps. Test whether taking a high or low path can support visible passing. Compare with Pin field at comparable incoming speeds and field density; another forced slalom does not earn a separate Module.

Use the Showcase and Validator together. Include Burst 15, staggered arrivals and a range of incoming speeds. Examine completion, stalls, Dwell Time, changes in order and persistent entry-side advantages. Watch whether the result is legible at the race camera distance. Numerical acceptance thresholds remain to be agreed before implementation.

No Board rewrite is justified yet. Prototype compact versions with one entry and one exit. Check their actual Footprints against Board placement before granting Course Eligibility. A split path needs additional care because current progress projects marbles onto one route.

Blender follows the behavior experiment. Use simple surfaces first; add polished banks and scenery once the mechanism earns its place. A convincing model cannot establish that a race mechanic is useful.
