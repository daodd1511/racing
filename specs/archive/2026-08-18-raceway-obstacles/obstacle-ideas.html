Raceway obstacle ideas

Every diagram is a plan view — looking straight down at the 11 m-wide track bed, marbles rolling left to right. The current course has one obstacle shape repeated nine times; these are fifteen alternatives, grouped by what they cost to build.

track bed (11 m wide) side rails obstacle marble

Tier 1 — box colliders only, buildable today

1Funnel choke NOT SCHEDULED

Walls squeeze 11 m down to a 2.2 m throat. The spread-out field is forced into a single-file queue, so who exits first is decided by contact, not by lane. Cut from the build order; the vortex bowl now does the queueing.

2 walls/side · 18° · throat ≥ 2.2 m · at 30% & 62%

2Diamond pin field

A Plinko board: posts turned 45° so each hit splits a marble left or right. Three or four rows deep and any lane advantage is gone.

0.5 m posts · 1.6 m spacing · gaps ≥ 1.2 m · at 20–26%

3Ice & mud patches NOT SCHEDULED

Flat panels flush with the bed — nothing to hit. One lane is fast, the neighbouring lane is slow, and no marble chooses which it lands in. Cut from the build order: an invisible lane advantage reads poorly on camera.

friction 0.01 vs 0.85 · 3.2 × 8 m · at 42%

4Rumble strip

Cattle-grid bars only 5 cm tall. Nothing gets blocked; the whole pack gets shaken so clean rolling lines break up before the next module.

0.05 m tall · full width · 1.1 m apart

5Lane comb, unequal exits

Five parallel channels, each dumping into something different. Viewers can see which lane is winning before the marbles get there.

12 m long · channels ≥ 1.5 m · at 52%

6Staircase drop

The only side-view here. Five 0.22 m steps: a fast marble carries across two, a slow one falls into every riser and loses more — it stretches the pack out.

drop ≤ 0.25 m · 2.5 m tread · bed geometry change

Tier 2 — needs a shape union on TrackBox (one refactor unlocks all four)

7Cylinder bumper posts

Same scatter as the pins, but round: the deflection angle varies smoothly with how far off-centre the hit lands, instead of splitting two ways.

r 0.4 m · restitution 0.2 · replaces module 2

8Wave section

No barrier at all — the bed itself rolls. Marbles bunch on the climbs and stretch on the drops, and the chase camera pitches over each crest.

amplitude 0.3 m · rails must follow the displacement

9Vortex bowl

An 8 m banked bowl. Marbles spiral the rim and exit in an order unrelated to the one they entered in — the biggest shuffle available, best right before the finish.

scheduled: Phase C · centreline coils into a spiral so progress still advances

10Hanging balls

Spheres slung low from a gantry. They only touch the top of a passing marble, which sends it skittering sideways rather than stopping it.

r 0.55 m · centre 0.7 m above bed · static variant of 12

Tier 3 — needs deterministic motion (a pure function of frame index) or sensor logic

11Windmill paddle

Blades sweep the bed continuously. Marbles arrive in a stream and leave in batches; catch a blade on the wrong face and you get swept backwards.

ω ≈ 1.6 rad/s · angle = f(frameIndex), never wall-clock

12Pendulum gate

A timing gate. The swing repeats exactly the same way every race; when a marble arrives does not — so neighbours get very different treatment.

period 2.4 s · ±0.9 rad · sin(frameIndex · dt)

13Traffic-light gates

Three barriers rising and falling on staggered cycles, splitting the field into waves. In last mode it visibly strands the stragglers.

ramp ≥ 0.4 s or Rapier resolves the overlap explosively

14Boost & drag pads

Sensor zones, not solids: an impulse is applied while a marble is inside. The only module here that can speed the race up if duration runs long.

setSensor(true) + applyImpulse along the tangent

15Rotating turntable

A disc set flush into the bed, spinning slowly. It drags whatever lands on it sideways into a rail or across a lane divider. Calm to watch, vicious.

ω ≈ 1.2 rad/s · friction 0.6 · flush within a millimetre

Build order

StepModulesWhy this order
ADiamond pin field, rumble stripBiggest change in race character, zero architectural cost
BShape union → cylinder posts, wave sectionOne refactor, then two modules land cheaply
CProgress hardening → vortex bowlWidest blast radius: it rewrites what ranking and the camera read
DMotion function → windmill, traffic gatesNew machinery; needs the frame-index contract first
ETuning and coverageLast, so it runs against finished geometry
Three constraints that decide the tiers
  1. The replay records marble transforms only, so any moving part must be a pure function of frame index shared by the simulation and the renderer — otherwise playback desyncs from the race that was run.
  2. Colliders are cuboid-only today. Everything round costs one discriminated-union refactor across definition.ts, colliders.ts and createRaceScene.ts.
  3. trackStress.test.ts asserts marbles stay under 0.6 m above the bed and inside halfWidth + 1.2. Modules 6, 8 and 9 break that by design and need a per-section allowance, not a global relaxation.
